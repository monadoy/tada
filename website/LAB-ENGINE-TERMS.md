These terms cover the **TADA Lab in-browser engine**: the files `engine.bin` and `engine.json` under
`website/docs/assets/lab/` and on the published site. They do not change the terms of anything else in
the repository.

1. **What the engine is.** The engine is the TADA flight simulator, the Milan Malpensa and Milan Bergamo
   scenario configurations and the single-agent environment, compiled to one WebAssembly module. It is
   published only in encrypted form (AES-256-GCM with a 256-bit key) and runs in the browser once it is
   unlocked with an engine key. Nothing is uploaded: flights are computed on your own machine.
2. **Who can have a key.** Anyone with a genuine interest in trying the Lab may ask: researchers, students,
   air traffic controllers, engineers, reviewers. Tell us your role and why you would like to use it. Keys
   are issued by hand by the TADA team; there is no entitlement to one, and a request may be declined.
3. **The key is for you.** Use it yourself, on your own devices. Do not pass it on, post it, or embed it
   in software or a web page. Colleagues who want to use the Lab ask for their own key.
4. **The engine stays in the Lab.** Do not redistribute the unlocked module, and do not extract, decompile
   or reuse it outside the Lab page. The open route to the simulator and the environment is the
   repository itself, under its own terms.
5. **Research use only.** The Lab is a demonstration of research software. It is not an operational tool
   and must not be used for the provision of air traffic services or to support real operational
   decisions.
6. **As is.** The engine is shared as is, without warranty of any kind and without support obligations.
7. **Results.** If you publish or present results obtained with the Lab, cite the paper named on the home
   page of the site and say that the Lab was used.
8. **Keys can change.** The team may rotate the key at any time, after which earlier keys stop working. A
   key that was shared against these terms will not be replaced.
9. **Your details.** The request form sends nothing to the site. Your request reaches the team as an
   email from your own mail account. The team uses your name, organisation, role and message only to
   decide on the request and to reply, and does not pass them on.
